\chapter{From Langevin Dynamics to the Maxwell--Boltzmann Gas}
\label{ch:langevin-maxwell-boltzmann}

The diffusion equation resolves position after microscopic velocity memory has
decayed.  This chapter restores velocity through the underdamped Langevin
equation.  Thermal fluctuation and momentum relaxation produce the
Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution.  The same dynamics becomes ballistic at short
time and Brownian at long time.

\section{Underdamped Langevin dynamics}

Let a particle of mass $m$ and signed charge $q_c$ have position $\bm R$ and
velocity $\bm V$.  A constant momentum-relaxation time $\tau$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \dd\bm R=\bm V\,\dd t,
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-position}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \dd\bm V
  =-\frac{\bm V}{\tau}\,\dd t
   +\frac{q_c}{m}\bm E\,\dd t
   +\sqrt{\frac{2\kb T}{m\tau}}\,\dd\bm W_t.
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-langevin}
\end{equation}
The damping term removes directed momentum.  The stochastic term restores the
thermal fluctuations lost through damping.  Their coefficients satisfy the
fluctuation--dissipation relation.

With $\bm E=\bm0$, each velocity component is an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process.
Its stationary covariance is
\begin{equation}
  \left\langle V_iV_j\right\rangle
  =\frac{\kb T}{m}\delta_{ij}.
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-velocity-covariance}
\end{equation}
The associated three-dimensional velocity density is
\begin{equation}
  f_{\mathrm{MB}}(\bm v)
  =\left(\frac{m}{2\pi\kb T}\right)^{3/2}
   \exp\!\left(-\frac{m|\bm v|^2}{2\kb T}\right).
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-maxwell-boltzmann}
\end{equation}
Integration over velocity direction gives the Maxwell speed density
\begin{equation}
  p(v)
  =4\pi v^2
   \left(\frac{m}{2\pi\kb T}\right)^{3/2}
   \exp\!\left(-\frac{mv^2}{2\kb T}\right),
  \qquad v\geq0.
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-maxwell-speed}
\end{equation}
The second moment gives equipartition,
\begin{equation}
  \frac12m\left\langle|\bm V|^2\right\rangle
  =\frac32\kb T.
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-equipartition}
\end{equation}
The Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution therefore emerges from the same stochastic
dynamics that produces Brownian diffusion.

\section{Velocity memory and the Brownian limit}

The equilibrium velocity autocorrelation in one Cartesian direction is
\begin{equation}
  \left\langle V(t)V(0)\right\rangle
  =\frac{\kb T}{m}e^{-t/\tau}.
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-velocity-autocorrelation}
\end{equation}
Integrating the velocity twice gives
\begin{equation}
  \left\langle[X(t)-X(0)]^2\right\rangle
  =2D\left[t-\tau\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)\right],
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-msd}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{D=\frac{\kb T\tau}{m}.}
  \label{eq:thermal-gas-diffusivity}
\end{equation}
For $t\ll\tau$, expansion of the exponential gives
$\langle\Delta X^2\rangle=(\kb T/m)t^2$.  The particle retains its velocity and
moves ballistically.  For $t\gg\tau$,
$\langle\Delta X^2\rangle=2Dt$ apart from a constant offset.  The overdamped
Brownian model is the long-time limit of the thermal velocity process.

\section{Chapter summary}

The underdamped Langevin equation couples position, velocity, damping, and
thermal noise.  Its stationary velocity distribution is Maxwell--Boltzmann and
satisfies equipartition.  Exponential loss of velocity memory produces a
ballistic mean-square displacement for $t\ll\tau$ and a diffusive
mean-square displacement for $t\gg\tau$.  The long-time diffusion coefficient
is $D=\kb T\tau/m$.  The next chapter applies an electric field to the same
velocity dynamics and obtains the Drude response.
